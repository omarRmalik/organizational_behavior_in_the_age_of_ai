# Organizational Behavior in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

**Omar R. Malik, PhD — Kettering University | MGMT-205 | Fall 2026**

---

## Overview

This repository contains the complete course design for an undergraduate Organizational Behavior (OB) course that examines how the behavior of people, groups, and organizations changes when humans and artificial intelligence build knowledge and make decisions together.

The course keeps the foundations of OB — personality, perception, learning, motivation, leadership, group decision making, diversity, and culture — and places each one in direct dialogue with current research on AI at work. Students read a textbook chapter, a classic OB article, and a recent AI article from journals such as the *Journal of Organizational Behavior*, *Academy of Management Journal*, *Administrative Science Quarterly*, *Journal of Management Studies*, and *Personnel Psychology*.

One question organizes the entire course:

> **How does organizational behavior change when humans and AI jointly develop knowledge and make decisions?**

---

## What Makes This Course Different

Most OB courses describe how people behave at work. Most AI courses describe what the technology can do. This course studies what happens *between* people and AI — and what that means for the theories managers rely on.

Students:

- Learn the core concepts of OB through a well-established textbook
- Read the classic studies behind those concepts and identify the assumptions they make about human knowers and human deciders
- Examine new evidence on how AI changes trust, learning, motivation, leadership, teamwork, fairness, and culture
- Write weekly reading reflections that propose how classic OB theories should change for human–AI work
- Study a real organization's human–AI work practices in a team project

Students leave the course able to explain OB, apply it, and question it.

---

## Course Structure

The course runs 10 weeks plus a finals week and moves up the three classic levels of OB analysis:

| Phase | Weeks | Level of Analysis | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundations | 1 | All levels | What OB is and why human–AI collaboration unsettles it |
| The Individual | 2–4 | Individual | Personality and trust, learning and perception, motivation and work design |
| The Group | 5–7 | Group | Leadership, group decision making, teams and psychological safety |
| The Organization | 8–9 | Organization | Diversity and algorithmic fairness, culture and organizational learning |
| Integration | 10–11 | All levels | Toward an OB of human–AI joint agency; presentations and final exam |

## The JKD Framework

The **Joint Knowing and Deciding (JKD) Framework** is the organizing structure of the course. It asks seven questions about any setting in which people and AI work together:

```
                 Human–AI Collaboration
                          │
     ┌────────────── KNOWING ──────────────┐
     │ 1. Who knows?                       │  Sources of knowledge
     │ 2. How is knowledge built?          │  Co-creation and validation
     └─────────────────────────────────────┘
     ┌────────────── DECIDING ─────────────┐
     │ 3. Who decides?                     │  Decision rights
     │ 4. Who is accountable?              │  Authority and responsibility
     └─────────────────────────────────────┘
     ┌────────────── CONSEQUENCES ─────────┐
     │ 5. How do people experience it?     │  Trust, motivation, stress, identity
     │ 6. Is it fair?                      │  Fairness, diversity, inclusion
     │ 7. Does the organization learn?     │  Culture, expertise, adaptation
     └─────────────────────────────────────┘
       asked at the Individual, Group, and Organization levels
```

Every reading, reflection, case discussion, and project milestone maps to one or more of these questions.

## Weekly Scholarly Dialogue

Week 1 introduces the course with Chapter 1 of the textbook. Beginning in Week 2, each week pairs a textbook chapter and a classic OB article with a recent AI article:

| Week | Theme | Classic OB | Contemporary AI |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Personality, Attitudes, and Trust in AI | Mayer, Davis & Schoorman (1995) — An integrative model of trust | Vuori et al. (2025) — Emotional and cognitive trust in AI |
| 3 | Learning and Perception with Algorithms | Nonaka (1994) — Organizational knowledge creation | Anthony (2021) — Black boxing algorithmic technologies |
| 4 | Motivation and Work Design | Hackman & Oldham (1976) — Job characteristics theory | Schulz et al. (2025) — AI adoption and job satisfaction |
| 5 | Leadership with AI | Graen & Uhl-Bien (1995) — Leader–member exchange | Liu et al. (2026) — AI-augmented leadership |
| 6 | Group Decision Making | Stasser & Titus (1985) — Hidden profiles | Zercher et al. (2025) — Team–AI decision quality |
| 7 | Teams, Trust, and Psychological Safety | Edmondson (1999) — Psychological safety | Erengin et al. (2025) — Trust formation toward AI teammates |
| 8 | Diversity, Inclusion, and Algorithmic Fairness | Ely & Thomas (2001) — Diversity perspectives | van den Broek et al. (2025) — Is there fairness in AI? |
| 9 | Culture, Expertise, and Organizational Learning | March (1991) — Exploration and exploitation | Faulconbridge et al. (2023) — How professionals adapt to AI |
| 10 | Toward an OB of Human–AI Joint Agency | Murray, Rhymer & Sirmon (2021) — Conjoined agency | Stelmaszak et al. (2025) — AI as an organizing capability |

Students approach every pairing with four questions: What does the classic theory explain? What does it assume about who knows and who decides? What does the AI study show? How would the theory change?

## Companion Course

This course is designed alongside [Strategic Management in the Age of Artificial Intelligence](https://github.com/omarRmalik/strategic_management_in_the_age_of_ai). Several readings are shared across the two courses, including March (1991), Murray, Rhymer & Sirmon (2021), and the JPMorganChase: Leadership in the Age of GenAI case. Where the strategy course asks how *organizations compete* with AI, this course asks how *people work* with it.
