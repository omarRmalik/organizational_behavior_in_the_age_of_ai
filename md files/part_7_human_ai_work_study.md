# Part VII
# The Human–AI Work Study

---

# Introduction

The Human–AI Work Study is the signature project of the course. Student teams examine how a real organization — or one department within it — uses AI in knowledge work and decisions, and how that use affects the people who work there.

Teams apply the JKD Framework at the individual, group, and organizational levels, and recommend changes that help people and AI work together more effectively and more fairly.

The project replaces the previous term project, which asked students to develop short- and long-term strategic plans. The new project keeps the focus on analyzing a real organization with course concepts, but shifts the lens from strategy to people.

---

# Learning Objectives

Through the Work Study, students will:

- apply OB theories to a real organization (CLO 2);
- analyze how AI changes the assumptions behind those theories (CLO 3);
- evaluate fairness, accountability, and well-being (CLO 4);
- and communicate evidence-based recommendations as a team (CLO 6).

---

# Project Overview

## Teams

Teams have three students, assigned by the instructor in Week 1. Students may submit up to two preferred teammates.

## Choosing an Organization

Teams choose an organization in which AI plays a visible role in how people know or decide. Examples:

- a hospital using AI in diagnosis or scheduling;
- a bank using AI in lending or fraud decisions;
- a manufacturer using AI in quality inspection or maintenance;
- a retailer using AI in customer service or workforce scheduling;
- a professional services firm using generative AI in research or drafting;
- a company using AI in hiring or performance evaluation;
- a co-op employer where a team member has worked (see confidentiality rules below).

Organizations must be approved by the instructor by the end of Week 2.

## Evidence

Teams may use:

- public sources: company reports, press coverage, job postings, employee reviews, case studies, and academic research;
- optional informational conversations with employees, with the person's consent and without collecting confidential information.

Teams must not enter confidential information, or personal information about identifiable people, into AI tools.

---

# Methodology

The project moves up the three levels of OB analysis, applying the seven JKD questions at each level.

## Milestone 1 — Individual Level (Week 3)

A 2-page memo analyzing how AI affects individual employees in the organization.

- **Who knows?** What do employees know that the AI does not?
- **How do people experience it?** Effects on trust, motivation, job design, and stress.
- One classic OB theory and one AI study applied to the evidence.

## Milestone 2 — Group Level (Week 6)

A 2-page memo analyzing how AI affects teams and leaders.

- **Who decides?** How decisions are shared among team members, leaders, and AI.
- **Who is accountable?** Who answers for AI-shaped decisions.
- Effects on team trust, psychological safety, and leadership relationships.

## Milestone 3 — Organization Level (Week 8)

A 2-page memo analyzing how AI affects the organization as a whole.

- **Is it fair?** Effects on diversity, inclusion, and fairness in people decisions.
- **Does the organization learn?** Effects on culture, expertise, and adaptation.

## Draft Report (Week 9)

A full draft for instructor feedback, integrating the three milestones and incorporating feedback.

## Presentation (Week 10)

A 12-minute team presentation followed by 5 minutes of questions from the class.

## Final Report (Week 11)

The final report, due during finals week.

Milestones receive written feedback and a grade. Together they account for 10% of the course grade.

---

# Required Final Deliverables

## Final Report (20%)

A report of no more than 8 pages (excluding references and appendices), single-spaced, one-inch margins, APA 7th edition citations, with:

1. **Executive summary** (half a page)
2. **The organization and its use of AI**
3. **Individual-level analysis**
4. **Group-level analysis**
5. **Organization-level analysis**
6. **OB theory update:** which classic OB theory most needs revision in this organization, and how
7. **Recommendations:** changes to job design, training, decision rights, trust calibration, and fairness safeguards
8. **References**
9. **AI disclosure statement**

## Presentation (10%)

A 12-minute presentation in which every team member speaks, followed by questions. Presentations are evaluated on analysis, evidence, recommendations, delivery, and response to questions.

---

# Peer Evaluation

At the end of the term, each student confidentially rates each teammate's contribution. When peer evaluations show a clear and consistent pattern of unequal contribution, the instructor may adjust an individual's project grade up or down by as much as one letter grade. Students experiencing team problems should contact the instructor early.

---

# Assessment Criteria

| Criterion | What Is Evaluated |
|---|---|
| OB Understanding | Accurate use of OB concepts and theories |
| Evidence | Credible, well-documented sources; verified AI contributions |
| Analysis | Clear application of the JKD Framework at all three levels |
| Theory Update | A thoughtful, evidence-based proposal for revising a classic theory |
| Ethics | Serious attention to fairness, accountability, and well-being |
| Recommendations | Specific, feasible, and linked to the analysis |
| Communication | Clear, well-organized writing and presentation |

Full rubrics appear in Part VIII.

# Professional Standards

- Treat the organization and its people with respect.
- Do not misrepresent yourself as working on behalf of the organization.
- Obtain consent before any conversation and honor requests for anonymity.
- Do not collect or share confidential information.
- Distinguish clearly between evidence and speculation.

# Capstone Reflection

The Human–AI Work Study asks students to do what managers increasingly must do: look past the technology to the people using it, and design work so that people and AI know and decide well together.
