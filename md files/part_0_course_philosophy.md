# Part 0
# Course Philosophy

> *"The central task in organizations is working with people. Increasingly, that work includes machines that know things and recommend decisions."*

---

# Why This Course Exists

Organizational behavior studies how people think, feel, and act at work — alone, in groups, and within organizations. For decades, the discipline has explained why some employees are motivated and others disengaged, why some teams make excellent decisions and others make poor ones, why some leaders build trust and others lose it, and why some organizations learn while others repeat their mistakes.

These explanations remain essential.

However, nearly all of them were developed in workplaces where only people held knowledge and only people made decisions.

That assumption no longer holds.

Today, an employee drafts a report with a generative AI assistant. A recruiter screens applicants with a predictive algorithm. A team debates a recommendation produced by a model none of them can fully explain. A manager receives an AI-generated summary of each employee's performance. A professional must explain to a client a decision that software, not the professional, made.

In each of these moments, knowledge is being built *jointly* by humans and AI, and decisions are being made *jointly* by humans and AI.

Organizational behavior must account for what happens in those moments.

---

# The Central Question

This course is organized around a single question.

> **How does organizational behavior change when humans and AI jointly develop knowledge and make decisions?**

Traditional OB courses ask:

- What motivates people at work?
- How do groups make decisions?
- What makes leaders effective?
- How do organizations build inclusive and learning cultures?

These remain important questions, and students will learn the established answers.

This course then asks a second question of every topic:

> **What does this theory assume about who knows and who decides — and what happens to it when AI becomes part of knowing and deciding?**

---

# Two Processes at the Heart of Organizational Life

Almost everything OB studies depends on two processes.

**Knowing.** Organizations run on knowledge: what employees perceive, learn, remember, and share. Classic OB treats knowledge as something people create through experience, conversation, and reflection (Nonaka, 1994) under the limits of bounded rationality (Simon, 1991).

**Deciding.** Organizations act through decisions: who is hired, how work is designed, which option a team chooses, how performance is evaluated. Classic OB treats decisions as human judgments shaped by personality, perception, group dynamics, and leadership.

AI now participates in both processes. It produces knowledge people did not produce, and it makes or shapes decisions people used to make alone. Research in the leading OB journals shows that this changes trust (Vuori et al., 2025), learning (Anthony, 2021), job satisfaction (Schulz et al., 2025), leadership (Liu et al., 2026), team decisions (Zercher et al., 2025), fairness (van den Broek et al., 2025), and professional expertise (Faulconbridge et al., 2023).

The course is built on these two processes. Part IV develops them into the Joint Knowing and Deciding (JKD) Framework.

---

# Artificial Intelligence Is Not the Subject

This is not a course about how AI works.

Students do not need programming experience. They will not build models.

The subject is people — how their behavior, relationships, and organizations change when AI becomes a coworker, an advisor, an evaluator, or a decision maker.

AI is the *context*. Organizational behavior is the *content*.

---

# A Dialogue Between Classic and Contemporary Research

Each week places three sources side by side:

1. **A textbook chapter** that explains the core OB concepts.
2. **A classic article** in which a foundational OB theory was introduced or tested.
3. **A contemporary AI article**, usually from the *Journal of Organizational Behavior*, *Academy of Management Journal*, *Administrative Science Quarterly*, *Journal of Management Studies*, or *Personnel Psychology*, that examines the same topic when AI is involved.

The goal is not to decide that the classic theory is wrong. Often it holds up remarkably well. Schulz et al. (2025), for example, use job characteristics theory — nearly fifty years old — to explain why AI adoption both raises and lowers job satisfaction.

The goal is to see *exactly where* the classic theory holds, where it bends, and where it needs new ideas.

---

# Learning by Working With AI

Students will not only read about human–AI collaboration. They will experience it.

Each week includes a short **Human–AI Learning Lab**, in which students complete an OB task together with an AI tool, and a written **reflection** on how the collaboration changed what they knew and what they decided.

These labs turn the central question of the course into direct experience. Students notice when they trusted AI too much or too little, when AI improved a decision and when it narrowed it, and when their own judgment mattered most.

---

# Learning Like an Organizational Analyst

The course culminates in the **Human–AI Work Study**, a team project in which students examine how a real organization uses AI in knowledge work and decisions. Teams analyze the organization at the individual, group, and organizational levels, and recommend changes to job design, training, decision rights, and fairness safeguards.

The project asks students to do what OB professionals, HR leaders, and managers increasingly must do: understand how people and AI actually work together, and design work so that both perform well.

---

# Responsible and Ethical Behavior

Ethics runs through every topic in this course.

When AI shapes hiring, evaluation, and promotion, fairness becomes an organizational design question. When AI shares in decisions, accountability can become unclear. When AI monitors work, autonomy and well-being are at stake.

Students will examine these issues not as a separate unit but as part of every week's discussion.

---

# The Vision of This Course

Students should leave this course able to:

- explain the core theories of organizational behavior;
- analyze how people, groups, and organizations work with AI;
- judge when classic theories still apply and when they must change;
- use AI thoughtfully as a partner in their own learning and decisions;
- and help organizations design human–AI work that is effective, fair, and humane.

The future workplace will not be humans *or* AI.

It will be humans *and* AI — knowing and deciding together.

This course prepares students to understand, lead, and improve that workplace.
