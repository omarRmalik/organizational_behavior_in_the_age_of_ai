# Assessment, Rubrics, and Standards

---

# Philosophy of Assessment

Assessment in this course rewards three things: understanding OB, applying it to real human–AI work, and thinking critically about how it should change.

Compared with previous offerings, which weighted exams at 60%, the course now places the majority of the grade on work that develops these skills over time: weekly reflections and the Human–AI Work Study. Exams remain, in person, to ensure every student masters the core concepts independently.

# Assignment Rubrics

## Class Participation

Participation is graded on case discussion days on a 0–3 scale per class.

| Score | Level | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | No substantive comment | Does not add to the discussion; repeats others; superficial or irrelevant; not listening, interrupting, or hostile |
| 1–2 | Straightforward comment | Adds to understanding; responds adequately; links concepts to the discussion; engages constructively with classmates |
| 3 | Insightful comment | Significantly improves understanding; shows deep thinking; connects the case to OB theory and the course's central question |

Students who find it difficult to speak in group discussions should talk with the instructor early in the term.

---

## Weekly Reading Reflections

There are nine reflections (Weeks 2–10). Each is scored out of 10. The best 8 of 9 count.

| Criterion | Points | Excellent | Developing |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Reading Connection | 4 | Accurately explains the classic theory, its assumptions about who knows and who decides, and what the AI study shows | Summarizes the readings without connecting them |
| OB Theory Update | 5 | A clear, specific, evidence-based revision to the classic theory | Vague claim that "AI changes things" |
| AI Disclosure | 1 | Complete and honest | Missing or incomplete |

## Examinations

**Midterm (Week 5):** Multiple-choice and short-answer questions covering Weeks 1–4. Questions focus on OB concepts and their application to workplace situations, including situations involving AI.

**Final (Finals week):** Multiple-choice and short-answer questions, cumulative with emphasis on Weeks 5–10.

Exams are taken in person without AI tools.

---

# Late Work

Reflections and milestones submitted late lose 10% per day, up to three days, after which they receive no credit unless an extension was arranged in advance. The final report cannot be accepted after the end of finals week without an approved incomplete.

---

# Professional Standards

All written work should be clear, well-organized, and free of avoidable errors, and should use APA 7th edition citations. Team presentations should reflect professional preparation and respect for the audience.

---

# Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence

Every reflection, milestone, and report includes an AI disclosure statement (Part IX). Honest disclosure is never penalized. Failing to disclose substantive AI use is treated as an academic integrity issue.

---

# Feedback and Continuous Improvement

Students receive written feedback on each reflection and milestone, typically within one week. Feedback on milestones is designed to be used in the next stage of the project.

---

# Assurance of Learning

Direct measures of learning are drawn from:

- exam item analysis (Knowledge — CLO 1);
- the final report's analysis criteria (Reasoning — CLO 2, CLO 3);
- the reflection rubric's theory update criterion (CLO 3);
- the final report's ethics criterion and the Week 8 reading reflection (Ethics — CLO 4);
- the reflection rubric and AI disclosure (Responsible AI use — CLO 5);
- the presentation rubric and peer evaluations (Communication and Teamwork — CLO 6).

Results are reviewed after each offering to improve the course.
