# Part IV
# The Joint Knowing and Deciding (JKD) Framework

---

# Introduction

Organizational behavior has always studied two things at once: the **processes** through which work gets done — perceiving, learning, communicating, deciding — and the **consequences** of those processes for people and organizations — motivation, satisfaction, well-being, fairness, performance, and learning.

When AI enters the workplace, it enters the processes first. It contributes knowledge, and it contributes to decisions. The consequences follow.

The **Joint Knowing and Deciding (JKD) Framework** gives students a consistent way to analyze any setting in which people and AI work together. It is used in every class discussion, lab, case, and project milestone.

---

# The Framework

The JKD Framework asks seven questions, grouped into three clusters, at each of the three levels of OB analysis.

```
                        HUMAN–AI COLLABORATION
                                 │
        ┌────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┐
        ▼                        ▼                        ▼
     KNOWING                 DECIDING               CONSEQUENCES
  1. Who knows?          3. Who decides?        5. How do people experience it?
  2. How is knowledge    4. Who is              6. Is it fair?
     built?                 accountable?        7. Does the organization learn?
        │                        │                        │
        └────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┘
                                 ▼
              INDIVIDUAL  ·  GROUP  ·  ORGANIZATION
```

---

# Cluster 1 — Knowing

## Question 1 — Who knows?

**Focus:** Sources of knowledge.

Classic OB assumes knowledge lives in people: in their experience, skills, and tacit understanding. AI adds a new source that is fast, broad, and pattern-based, but often cannot explain itself and lacks context.

Key questions:

- What does the person know that the AI does not, and vice versa?
- Is the AI's knowledge visible and explainable, or a black box?
- Which knowledge is tacit and cannot be handed to AI?

**Core readings:** Simon (1991); Nonaka (1994); Anthony (2021).

---

## Question 2 — How is knowledge built?

**Focus:** Co-creation and validation of knowledge.

Knowledge is not just stored; it is created, combined, and checked. When people and AI work together, new knowledge can emerge that neither would have produced alone — or errors can spread because no one checks.

Key questions:

- How do human and AI contributions combine?
- Who verifies the result, and how?
- Does AI help people learn, or let them stop learning?

**Core readings:** Nonaka (1994); Anthony (2021); Jia et al. (2024); Kim et al. (2025).

---

# Cluster 2 — Deciding

## Question 3 — Who decides?

**Focus:** Decision rights.

Decisions may be made by a person with AI advice, by AI with human review, by both in sequence, or by AI alone. Shrestha, Ben-Menahem, and von Krogh (2019) call these different *decision-making structures*.

Key questions:

- Where does the AI's role end and the human's begin?
- When do people follow AI advice, and when do they override it?
- How does AI change what groups discuss and decide?

**Core readings:** Stasser & Titus (1985); Zercher et al. (2025); Lee et al. (2025); Liu et al. (2026).

---

## Question 4 — Who is accountable?

**Focus:** Authority and responsibility.

When a decision is shared, responsibility can become unclear. Professionals may remain accountable for decisions they no longer control.

Key questions:

- Who answers for a decision that AI shaped?
- How do experts maintain authority when AI decides?
- Does sharing decisions with AI change leaders' ethical behavior?

**Core readings:** Mayer, van den Broek, & Karačić (2025); Faulconbridge et al. (2023); He et al. (2025).

---

# Cluster 3 — Consequences

## Question 5 — How do people experience it?

**Focus:** Trust, motivation, stress, and identity.

People react to AI with thoughts *and* feelings. They may trust it too much or too little, find their work more or less meaningful, and feel supported or controlled.

Key questions:

- Is trust in the AI calibrated to its actual reliability?
- How does AI change autonomy, feedback, skill variety, and meaning in work?
- Does AI reduce or increase stress?

**Core readings:** Mayer, Davis, & Schoorman (1995); Vuori et al. (2025); Hackman & Oldham (1976); Schulz et al. (2025); Klonek & Parker (2025); Erengin et al. (2025).

---

## Question 6 — Is it fair?

**Focus:** Fairness, diversity, and inclusion.

AI in hiring, evaluation, and promotion can reduce some human biases and introduce new ones. Fairness is not only a property of the algorithm; it is shaped by how people use the algorithm's outputs.

Key questions:

- Who benefits and who is harmed by the AI's role?
- Which definition of fairness is being applied, and by whom?
- How does the organization's diversity perspective shape its use of AI?

**Core readings:** Ely & Thomas (2001); van den Broek et al. (2025); Zhang et al. (2023); Triana & Upadhyay (2026).

---

## Question 7 — Does the organization learn?

**Focus:** Culture, expertise, and adaptation.

Organizations must both explore new possibilities and exploit what they already know. AI can speed learning or lock in old patterns. Culture determines whether people speak up about AI's mistakes.

Key questions:

- Does AI push the organization toward exploration or exploitation?
- Do people feel psychologically safe to question AI?
- How are professional roles and expertise changing?

**Core readings:** March (1991); Edmondson (1999); Faulconbridge et al. (2023); Stelmaszak et al. (2025).

---

# Three Levels of Analysis

Each question can be asked at three levels:

| JKD Question | Individual | Group | Organization |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Who knows? | What do I know vs. the AI? | What does the team know collectively with AI? | Where does organizational knowledge reside? |
| 2. How is knowledge built? | Am I learning or outsourcing? | Does AI help pool team knowledge? | Is AI-generated knowledge captured and checked? |
| 3. Who decides? | When do I follow AI advice? | How do teams decide with AI members? | What decision rights are given to AI? |
| 4. Who is accountable? | Am I responsible for AI-shaped choices? | Who owns team decisions? | What governance assigns responsibility? |
| 5. How do people experience it? | Trust, motivation, stress | Team trust and cohesion | Climate and employee well-being |
| 6. Is it fair? | Am I treated fairly by AI? | Are team members treated equally? | Are policies and systems fair across groups? |
| 7. Does the organization learn? | Do I keep developing expertise? | Can the team speak up about AI? | Does the culture support adaptation? |

# OB Theory Update

Each weekly reflection closes with an OB Theory Update that uses the framework:

> *"If [this week's classic theory] were rewritten for workplaces where people and AI build knowledge and make decisions together, I would change… because [evidence]. This matters most for JKD Question __ at the __ level."*

---

# Concluding Perspective

The JKD Framework is not a checklist to be completed once. It is a habit of mind.

Whenever students encounter AI at work — as employees, managers, or customers — they should be able to ask: *Who knows? How is knowledge built? Who decides? Who is accountable? How do people experience it? Is it fair? Does the organization learn?*

Those seven questions are the practical core of organizational behavior in the age of AI.
