# Part III
# Intellectual Foundations

---

# Overview

The course is built on a deliberate dialogue between two bodies of research:

1. **The classic OB canon** — studies that established how people trust, learn, stay motivated, lead, decide in groups, work across differences, and build organizational knowledge.
2. **The AI-at-work canon** — recent studies, mostly published since 2021 in leading OB and management journals, on what happens to those same processes when AI participates.

Both are needed. The classic canon tells students what to look for. The AI canon tells them what is changing.

---

# Learning Philosophy

Students read research articles to answer three questions:

1. What does this study explain about behavior at work?
2. What does it assume about **who knows** and **who decides**?
3. What happens to those assumptions when AI becomes part of knowing and deciding?

Undergraduate students are not expected to master every methodological detail. They are expected to find the central argument, the key evidence, and the underlying assumptions. Guided reading questions are provided each week.

# The Classic OB Canon

| Theme | Reading | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Bounded Rationality | Simon, H. A. (1991). Bounded rationality and organizational learning. *Organization Science, 2*(1), 125–134. | People decide and learn within cognitive limits — limits AI may extend or reshape. |
| Trust | Mayer, R. C., Davis, J. H., & Schoorman, F. D. (1995). An integrative model of organizational trust. *Academy of Management Review, 20*(3), 709–734. | Defines trust through ability, benevolence, and integrity — but can a system be benevolent? |
| Personality | Barrick, M. R., & Mount, M. K. (1991). The Big Five personality dimensions and job performance: A meta-analysis. *Personnel Psychology, 44*(1), 1–26. | Links stable traits to performance; now informs AI-based selection. |
| Knowledge Creation | Nonaka, I. (1994). A dynamic theory of organizational knowledge creation. *Organization Science, 5*(1), 14–37. | Knowledge is created as tacit and explicit knowledge convert — AI works mostly with the explicit. |
| Work Design | Hackman, J. R., & Oldham, G. R. (1976). Motivation through the design of work: Test of a theory. *Organizational Behavior and Human Performance, 16*(2), 250–279. | Autonomy, feedback, and task identity drive motivation — all reshaped by AI. |
| Leadership | Graen, G. B., & Uhl-Bien, M. (1995). Relationship-based approach to leadership: Development of leader-member exchange (LMX) theory. *The Leadership Quarterly, 6*(2), 219–247. | Leadership as a relationship — what happens when leaders collaborate with AI? |
| Group Decisions | Stasser, G., & Titus, W. (1985). Pooling of unshared information in group decision making. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 48*(6), 1467–1478. | Groups discuss what members already share — can AI surface what they don't? |
| Psychological Safety | Edmondson, A. (1999). Psychological safety and learning behavior in work teams. *Administrative Science Quarterly, 44*(2), 350–383. | Teams learn when members feel safe to speak up — including about AI outputs. |
| Diversity | Ely, R. J., & Thomas, D. A. (2001). Cultural diversity at work. *Administrative Science Quarterly, 46*(2), 229–273. | How organizations frame diversity shapes its effects — AI encodes such frames. |
| Organizational Learning | March, J. G. (1991). Exploration and exploitation in organizational learning. *Organization Science, 2*(1), 71–87. | Organizations balance learning new things and refining old ones — AI can tip the balance. |
| Agency | Murray, A., Rhymer, J., & Sirmon, D. G. (2021). Humans and technology: Forms of conjoined agency in organizations. *Academy of Management Review, 46*(3), 552–571. | A bridge between classic and AI-era OB: humans and technology act together. |

Open-access articles are preferred so all students can read them without cost.

# Reading Strategy

For each pair of readings, students answer four questions.

## Question 1

What behavior does the classic theory explain, and what is its central claim?

## Question 2

What does the theory assume about who holds knowledge and who makes decisions?

## Question 3

What does the AI study find, and which assumption does it support, challenge, or change?

## Question 4

How would you revise the theory for workplaces where people and AI know and decide together?

# Intellectual Contribution of the Course

The course invites undergraduate students into an active research conversation. By the end of the term, each student will have written nine short theory updates — proposals for how classic OB ideas should change when humans and AI know and decide together. The strongest of these become the conceptual core of their team's Human–AI Work Study.
