# Part IX
# Responsible AI, Academic Integrity, and Professional Conduct

> *"Artificial intelligence changes how we work. It does not change our responsibility for the quality, integrity, and consequences of our decisions."*

---

# Introduction

In this course, AI is both a subject of study and a learning partner. Students are welcome to use AI in their work, within the guidelines below.

AI is treated neither as a prohibited technology nor as a substitute for thinking. Its outputs must be questioned, verified, and used responsibly. The purpose of this policy is to make expectations clear and to protect the integrity of every student's learning.

---

# Guiding Principles

## Principle 1 — Human Judgment Remains Primary

AI can generate information, summarize readings, and suggest ideas. Students remain responsible for every statement, analysis, citation, and conclusion they submit.

## Principle 2 — Transparency Builds Trust

Students disclose substantive AI use in every graded written assignment. Disclosure is a sign of professional integrity, not weakness, and honest disclosure is never penalized.

## Principle 3 — Verification Is Required

AI can produce information that is inaccurate, outdated, or fabricated. Students verify AI-generated material against the textbook, assigned readings, and other credible sources. Unverified AI output is never treated as evidence.

## Principle 4 — Scholarship Requires Attribution

AI is not a scholarly source. Students cite the original articles, books, and documents on which their work relies, and cite only sources they have actually consulted.

## Principle 5 — Privacy and Confidentiality Matter

Organizational behavior involves real people. Students must never enter into AI tools:

- personal information about identifiable people, including classmates, co-op colleagues, or interviewees;
- confidential information about their project organization or co-op employer.

## Principle 6 — Fairness and Well-Being Matter

As this course shows, AI can affect how people are hired, evaluated, and treated. Students should consider fairness, accountability, autonomy, and well-being whenever they recommend how AI should be used at work.

---

# Appropriate Uses of Artificial Intelligence

Students are encouraged to use AI to:

- explain difficult concepts in the textbook or readings;
- generate practice questions for exams;
- brainstorm ideas and alternative explanations;
- find gaps or weaknesses in their own arguments;
- improve the clarity of their writing;
- prepare presentation drafts.

---

# Inappropriate Uses of Artificial Intelligence

The following are inconsistent with the expectations of this course:

- submitting AI-generated work without meaningful personal contribution;
- fabricating citations, quotations, or evidence;
- citing sources that have not been read;
- using AI during examinations;
- entering personal or confidential information into AI tools;
- using AI to write a reading reflection without reading the assigned articles;
- failing to disclose substantive AI use.

---

# AI Disclosure Statement

Every reflection, milestone, report, and presentation includes a brief disclosure. A suggested format:

> **AI Disclosure:** I used [tool] to [purpose, e.g., explain a concept, suggest counterarguments, edit for clarity]. I verified [what] against [sources]. All analysis and conclusions are my own, and I take full responsibility for the accuracy of this submission.

If no AI was used, students write: *"No AI tools were used in preparing this work."*

---

# Prompt Documentation

Students are encouraged to keep a record of significant AI interactions used in their reflections and project work. These records support the AI disclosure statement and may be requested by the instructor.

---

# Academic Integrity

All submitted work must be the student's own. Fabricating citations, presenting AI-generated or others' work as one's own, misrepresenting sources, or collaborating on individual work without permission constitutes academic dishonesty and will be addressed according to Kettering University policy.

---

# Intellectual Property and Course Materials

Harvard Business School cases and copyrighted articles are provided for enrolled students' personal study only. Students may not upload them to AI tools that retain or train on submitted content, or share them outside the course.

---

# Attendance and Professional Conduct

This course is discussion-intensive and meets in person. Students are expected to attend every class having completed the readings. If an exceptional circumstance prevents attendance, students should notify the instructor before class. There is no separate grade for attendance, but participation is graded on case discussion days and cannot be earned when absent.

Students are expected to listen respectfully, disagree constructively, and support classmates' learning, particularly during team work and discussions of sensitive topics such as diversity and fairness.

---

# Accessibility and Accommodations

Students who need accommodations because of a disability should contact the university's disability services office and provide documentation as early as possible. The instructor is committed to equitable access to all course materials and activities.

---

# University Policies

Students are responsible for reviewing Kettering University's standard syllabus policies, including those governing academic integrity, disability services, Title IX, student conduct, and grade appeals. These policies are incorporated by reference.

---

# Course Commitment

This course asks students to work with AI and to study what that work does to people. The same standards apply to both: be honest, verify what you rely on, respect others, and take responsibility for your decisions.
